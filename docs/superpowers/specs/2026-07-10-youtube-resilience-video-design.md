# YouTube Video Design: How I Rewrote My Story After Losing My Leg

## Overview

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Title** | How I Rewrote My Story After Losing My Leg |
| **Short Hook** | Your support group is your superpower — here's why |
| **Format** | Long-form YouTube (10–12 min) + Shorts clips cut from it |
| **Topic** | Resilience mindset shift: leaning into your support group and the things you love to write your new story |
| **Audience** | Universal — amputee experience as the lens, message applies to anyone navigating a major loss or setback |
| **Approach** | Story-first: raw personal narrative → framework revealed through story → community/movement ending |

---

## Video Structure

### Section 1 — The Raw Moment (0:00–1:30)

Open *in media res* — not the surgery, not the hospital, but the specific moment the old story broke. The first time you looked down and realized the person you were before wasn't coming back.

No intro, no channel branding yet. Just Travis in frame, telling the truth.

**Goal:** Hook the viewer emotionally in the first 30 seconds. Make them feel they already know what this moment feels like — even if their loss is different.

**Shorts clip opportunity:** This opening can be cut as a standalone Short.

---

### Section 2 — The Shift (1:30–4:30)

The mindset reframe. Not a sudden epiphany — show it arriving gradually through two sources:

- **Your support group:** Specific people, specific moments of being held up. Name them. Show what it actually looked like when someone showed up for you.
- **The things you love:** What you returned to that reminded you who you still are. Not who you were — who you *are*.

The framework ("lean into your people + your passions") lands *through* the story here, not as a lecture or a listicle.

**Goal:** Give the viewer a concrete, repeatable formula without it ever feeling like a formula.

**Shorts clip opportunity:** The "two sources" reveal is a strong standalone Short.

---

### Section 3 — Writing the New Story (4:30–8:00)

What it actually looks like to rebuild. Practical and personal — not generic advice. Your journey as proof of concept.

This is where the phrase "resilience mindset shift" gets named explicitly. Anchor it to a specific moment where you felt the new story beginning — not the finish line, but the first step.

**Goal:** Make the viewer believe their new story is already underway, even if they can't see it yet.

---

### Section 4 — The Challenge (8:00–end)

Direct address to camera. Movement ending — this is where the video becomes a call to action for something bigger than one person's story.

- Name what the viewer is carrying right now
- Deliver the challenge: *"Who in your life is your support group? What's the thing you love that you've been putting off? Start there. That's where your new story begins."*
- Call to action: share their own new story in the comments — build the community thread under this video

**Goal:** Leave the viewer with one concrete next step, not a feeling. The comments section becomes the movement.

**Shorts clip opportunity:** The challenge/CTA section cuts well as a Short with a direct hook.

---

| Short 3 | Section 4 (the challenge) | *"Nobody tells you THIS about starting over after a major loss"* |

---

## Tone & Style Notes

- **No generic motivational language.** Every line should be specific to Travis's actual experience.
- **Viewer is addressed as an equal**, not someone being coached from above.
- **The framework is implicit until Section 3** — let it emerge from the story, then name it.
- **Community is the thesis**, not an afterthought. Section 4 should feel like the beginning of something, not a wrap-up.
